## Domain non-goals (from issue)
- The sweep stays UX-only reconciliation per ADR-0002. Lazy check-on-read remains the authoritative
  expiry mechanism; this milestone changes **how the sweep locks, never what it does**.
- No alerting, dashboards, or log aggregation — the deliverable is an endpoint that tells the
  truth, not a monitoring stack.

## Constraints carried in from M7 (from the issue)
- **ADR-0010 binds T-001.** Gather the sweep's target ids with a **scalar projection only**.
  Materializing managed `EventSeat` entities to collect ids re-creates the identity-map defect that
  caused the double-booking in #12.
- **ADR-0011 must survive T-001.** The sweep still touches `event_seats` before `holds`, and
  `expireOverdueHolds()` still runs after `releaseExpiredHeldSeats()` in the same transaction.
- **ADR-0004 binds T-002.** A blanket `/actuator/**` permitAll would reintroduce the default-allow
  shape #14 removed, one path prefix over.

## Task ordering
T-001 → T-002 → T-003 → T-004 → T-005, and two of the edges are load-bearing:

- **T-001 first**, because it is the only live correctness defect in the milestone and everything
  after it is additive observability. It also touches nothing the other four touch, so an
  interruption between T-001 and T-002 leaves a coherent tree.
- **T-003 depends on T-002.** The Redis-non-fatal and Postgres-fatal assertions need an exposed,
  reachable health endpoint to assert against; without T-002's matcher the endpoint 401s and
  T-003's tests would be measuring the filter chain rather than the aggregate.
- **T-004 depends on T-002** for the URL it polls, and on T-003 for that URL being the *right*
  one (readiness, not the plain aggregate — see T-004).
- **T-005 last**, because Micrometer arrives transitively with T-002's starter whether or not it is
  exposed, so the decision is about exposure and custom instrumentation, not about a dependency.

  `spring-boot-micrometer-metrics`), **but `spring-boot-starter-actuator` 4.1.0 depends on

- `idx_event_seats_current_hold` (V10) is a **partial** index on `current_hold_id`, which is what

Verified during the grilling session, against the published Boot 4.1.0 artifacts:
- `management.endpoints.web.exposure.include` **already defaults to `['health']`**, so
  `/actuator/env` and `/actuator/heapdump` are not exposed out of the box. The explicit allowlist
  is a confirmation and a guard against future widening, not a fix for a live hole.
- `management.endpoint.health.probes.enabled` **already defaults to `true`** in Boot 4 (it was
  off-unless-Kubernetes in Boot 3). T-002's original packet told the builder to set it; verify
  before adding a redundant line.
- `management.endpoint.health.show-details` defaults to `'never'` and `show-components` to unset,
  so the endpoint returns a bare `{"status":"UP"}` by default — directly at odds with #18's
  requirement that the Redis indicator show its true state. Hence the `show-components=always`
  decision.
- `management.endpoint.health.validate-group-membership` defaults to `true`: a typo'd group member
  fails at **startup**. A free guard, and one of the arguments for configuring readiness by group.
- `management.health.redis.enabled` and `management.health.db.enabled` both default to `true`, so
  both indicators are auto-configured and both contribute to the aggregate unless composed otherwise.
- Boot's default `readiness` group is `readinessState` alone — **`db` is not a member unless named.**
  "Postgres is fatal to readiness" is therefore something that must be written down, not inherited.
- `StatusAggregator.getAggregateStatus(Set<Status>)` takes statuses with **no component names**
  (checked with `javap` against `spring-boot-health` 4.1.0), so a custom aggregator cannot ignore
  Redis while keeping Postgres fatal.
- `springdoc.show-actuator` defaults to `false`, so actuator paths stay out of `/v3/api-docs` and
  `OpenApiDocumentationTest`'s anonymous-401 walk will not enumerate them. A risk flagged in
  T-002's original packet, now closed with no action needed.
- Both contention throw sites are in `HoldService`: line 94 (Redis fail-fast, in `createHold`) and
  lines 135/139 (under the row lock, in `createHoldWithSeatsLocked`). **Line numbers go stale — the
  builder must re-derive them.**

## Two deliberate departures from issue #19's wording
Both are recorded in ADR-0013 and in T-004, and must be **noted when the issue is closed** rather
than quietly reinterpreted:

1. #19 says *"health reports UP with Redis stopped."* Under ADR-0013 the **parent**
   `/actuator/health` reports DOWN in that scenario; **readiness** reports UP. The verification's
   intent — do not evict a working instance — is fully met, and an honestly degraded aggregate is
   the better system.
2. #19 asks for a healthcheck on **both** the image and the compose service. It is declared once,
   in the `Dockerfile`; compose gets a pointer comment. Two copies of a healthcheck is a drift
   hazard with no benefit, since `depends_on: condition: service_healthy` reads the container's
   health status, which comes from the image.

## Decisions from grilling (2026-08-16)

### T-001 — the sweep's lock order
- **The fix shape issue #16 proposed is not a fix.** "Select the ids ordered, then
  `UPDATE ... WHERE id IN (:ids)`" changes nothing: the `SELECT` is unlocked so ordering it orders
  no locks, and the planner remains free to reorder the `UPDATE`'s `IN` list. That shape ships two
  statements, a comment claiming the bug is fixed, and the bug.
- **The ordering statement must be the one that takes the locks.** Native
  `SELECT id ... ORDER BY es.id FOR UPDATE OF es`, then the existing bulk `UPDATE`. Postgres puts
  the row-locking step above the sort, so locks are acquired ascending; the following `UPDATE` only
  re-touches rows the same transaction already holds, so its plan order stops mattering.
- **`FOR UPDATE OF es`, never bare `FOR UPDATE`** — the predicate joins `holds`, and a bare
  `FOR UPDATE` would take the `holds` locks in the same statement, violating ADR-0011 while fixing
  its residual.
- **Native, not JPQL**, because Hibernate will not reliably emit `FOR UPDATE` for a scalar JPQL
  projection. Still a scalar id projection, so ADR-0010 holds.
- **The test asserts lock order directly, not a staged deadlock.** Park a transaction on the *low*
  id seat; run the sweep; assert via `pg_locks` that while the sweep is blocked on the low seat it
  holds **no** lock on the high seat. A deadlock is a consequence of unordered locking; the
  ordering is the property. One background thread instead of four-way choreography, and it still
  genuinely fails pre-fix.
- **The test must assert its own premise** — that the unordered scan really does yield high-before-low
  — and fail loudly if that stops holding, or a future planner change turns it into a silent no-op.

### T-002/T-003 — the observability surface
- **Shared port 8080; no separate management port.** Boot gives a management port its own filter
  chain, which would mean two authorization postures where ADR-0004's whole claim is that there is
  one, defaulting to deny. See ADR-0012.
- **`exposure.include=health,metrics`.** No Prometheus registry — that is the first brick of the
  monitoring stack #19's non-goals rule out.
- **`permitAll` on the health group paths only, route by route. `/actuator/metrics` gets no matcher
  at all**, so deny-by-default authenticates it for free. The absent matcher *is* the mechanism.
- **`show-components=always` + `show-details=when-authorized`.** Anonymous callers get each
  indicator's UP/DOWN — which is what "the Redis indicator shows its true state" actually requires
  — without the database engine, Redis version, or container filesystem path that
  `show-details=always` would publish.
- **Redis is excluded from readiness by group composition, not by a custom indicator.**
  `readiness.include=readinessState,db`. A custom always-UP Redis indicator was rejected because,
  combined with `show-components=always`, it would report `redis: UP` while Redis was down. See
  ADR-0013.
- **A custom `StatusAggregator` cannot solve this** — verified against `spring-boot-health` 4.1.0:
  `getAggregateStatus(Set<Status>)` receives statuses with no component names, so it cannot ignore
  Redis while keeping Postgres fatal. It is the first thing anyone will reach for, and it does not exist.
- **Postgres-fatal is asserted behaviourally, not by breaking the datasource.** The context cannot
  boot without a database. Assert that the live readiness group contains `db` and not `redis`; state
  in the Javadoc that this proves composition, not aggregation.

### T-005 — metrics (revised 2026-08-16, after the initial decision was reopened)
- **Instrument the sweep, not hold contention.** Decision history, so it is not re-litigated:
  planning recommended defaults-only; the user chose a hold-contention counter; on re-examination
  the user switched to sweep-volume metrics. The reasoning for the switch *is* the "recorded rather
  than defaulted into" deliverable.
- **Why the sweep wins.** (1) The contention counter's job was to prove the two-tier design works,
  but `HoldRedisUnavailableRaceIntegrationTest` and `NoOversellIntegrationTest` already prove that
  continuously and fail the build when it stops being true — it would have re-proved a proven thing
  in a form that only helps if someone looks. (2) Sweep volume tests a real prediction of ADR-0002:
  if lazy check-on-read is authoritative, the sweep should usually find little to do; routinely
  large batches mean the prediction is wrong, and nothing else can tell you that. (3) The row counts
  already exist and are discarded, and `HoldSweepService` is a scheduled job rather than the method
  where three separate M7 defects hid.
- **Two `DistributionSummary` instances, no tags:** `seatvault.sweep.seats.reclaimed` and
  `seatvault.sweep.holds.expired`. Summary rather than `Counter` because "500 seats reclaimed"
  cannot distinguish 500 healthy sweeps of one seat from one backlog spike of 500 — and that
  distinction is the whole question. Max batch size comes free from the same call.
- **Record every sweep including the zeros**, because "how often does the sweep find anything at
  all" is precisely the ADR-0002 question. Commented, or a future reader assumes it was an oversight.
- **A named `SweepMetrics` collaborator, not raw `MeterRegistry` calls** — metric-name typos are
  silent, yielding a permanently empty time series and no error anywhere.
- **T-005 now depends on T-001**, which changes `releaseExpiredHeldSeats`'s shape. Instrumentation
  must not reorder or split the two statements ADR-0011 requires to stay in one transaction.
- **Nothing can scrape it, and that is accepted** (ADR-0012). Tokens are 10-minute non-revocable
  user JWTs (ADR-0005). The tempting one-line "fix" — `permitAll` on `/actuator/metrics` — is
  explicitly recorded as the wrong move.
- **The rejected hold-contention counter's design is preserved in T-005**, so revisiting it does not
  mean re-deriving it.
