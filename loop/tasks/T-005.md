# T-005 — Sweep-volume metrics (`seatvault.sweep.*`)
Milestone: M8 (#19)   Status: pending
Depends on: T-001 (it changes the shape of `releaseExpiredHeldSeats`), T-002 (the exposure allowlist)

## Description
Issue #19's deliverable was *"an explicit decision on metrics scope (Micrometer defaults vs. a
hold-contention counter) — either is acceptable, but recorded rather than defaulted into."*

**Decision, after two rounds of discussion: instrument the sweep, not hold contention.**

History, so nobody re-litigates it: planning first recommended defaults-only; the user chose the
hold-contention counter; on re-examination the user switched to this. The reasoning for the switch
is below and is the substance of the "recorded rather than defaulted into" deliverable.

## Why the sweep, and not hold contention
Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It measures something genuinely unobserved.** The hold-contention counter's job was to prove
   the two-tier design works — but `HoldRedisUnavailableRaceIntegrationTest` and
   `NoOversellIntegrationTest` already prove that continuously, and they fail the build when it
   stops being true. The counter would have re-proved a proven thing in a form that only helps if
   somebody happens to look. Sweep volume is not proven or observed anywhere.
2. **It answers a real question about ADR-0002.** That ADR makes lazy check-on-read authoritative
   and the sweep merely UX-only reconciliation. That is a *prediction*: the sweep should usually
   find little to do, because a reader normally reconciles an expired hold first. If sweeps
   routinely reclaim large batches, the prediction is wrong — either lazy expiry is not firing
   where expected, or there is a class of seat nobody reads. **Nothing else in the system can tell
   you this.**
3. **It is far cheaper and safer.** The two row counts already exist and are discarded today.
   `HoldSweepService` is a scheduled job, not the hot path — unlike `HoldService.createHold`, where
   three separate defects hid during M7. No cardinality risk, no code in a concurrency-critical
   method.

## SETTLED — do not re-decide

**Two `DistributionSummary` instances, recorded once per sweep, no tags.**

- `seatvault.sweep.seats.reclaimed` — from `eventSeatRepository.releaseExpiredHeldSeats(now)`
- `seatvault.sweep.holds.expired` — from `holdRepository.expireOverdueHolds(now)`

**Why `DistributionSummary` and not `Counter`:** a counter reading "500 seats reclaimed" cannot
distinguish 500 sweeps of one seat (healthy) from one sweep of 500 (a backlog spike) — and that
distinction is the entire question being asked. A summary gives count, total, mean and **max** from
the same single call, so the worst batch the system has ever seen is visible. A `Counter` is
simpler and would be accepted if the summary turns out awkward, but say so rather than silently
downgrading.

**Record every sweep, including the zeros.** An idle sweep records `0`. That is deliberate: "how
often does the sweep find anything at all" is precisely the ADR-0002 question, and skipping zeros
would make the mean describe only the sweeps that found work. Put this in a comment — it changes
what the numbers mean, and a future reader will otherwise assume zeros were an oversight.

**No tags at all.** Nothing here varies by seat, event, or user, so the cardinality trap that
governs custom metrics does not arise. Keep it that way.

**Where it lives:** a small constructor-injected collaborator (e.g. `SweepMetrics`) with one method
taking both counts, so the metric names are written once. Metric-name typos are silent — a
misspelling yields a permanently empty time series and no error anywhere — which is the same reason
the rejected contention counter was also going to get a named collaborator rather than raw
`MeterRegistry` calls at the call sites.

## Interaction with T-001 — read this
T-001 changes `releaseExpiredHeldSeats` from one statement into a locking ordered projection plus a
bulk `UPDATE`. **Do T-001 first**, then instrument whatever shape it left behind. Two notes:

- The projection returns `List<Long>`, so its `size()` and the `UPDATE`'s row count describe the
  same set — every id was locked by the projection, so nothing can change between them. Record one
  number, not both, and say which.
- Do not let the instrumentation change the transaction. `HoldSweepService.sweepExpiredHolds()` is
  `@Transactional`, and ADR-0011 requires `expireOverdueHolds()` to keep running after
  `releaseExpiredHeldSeats()` in the same transaction. Recording metrics must not reorder, split,
  or wrap those two calls.

## Acceptance criteria
- `SweepMetrics` (or equivalent) exists, is constructor-injected (CLAUDE.md: no `@Autowired`
  fields), and is the only place the metric names are written.
- `HoldSweepService` records both quantities once per sweep, including zeros, with the ADR-0002
  reasoning in a comment.
- **A test that observes the metrics recording real values, and that fails if the recording is
  removed.** `HoldIntegrationTest#sweepReclaimsAnExpiredHeldSeatAndExpiresItsHold` already builds
  exactly one expired hold and calls `sweepExpiredHolds()` directly, so asserting "one seat, one
  hold" is deterministic and needs no concurrency. **LESSONS: prove the guard by deleting the
  recording and watching the test fail.**
- A second assertion that an **idle** sweep records `0` rather than recording nothing — otherwise
  the zero-recording decision above is untested and someone will "optimise" it away.
- `management.endpoints.web.exposure.include` includes `metrics` (T-002), and `/actuator/metrics`
  remains authenticated via the absence of a matcher (ADR-0012).
- No regression in `HoldIntegrationTest`, and no change to sweep behaviour — ADR-0002 and ADR-0011
  both require the sweep to do exactly what it did before.
- Full `./mvnw test` green.

## Nothing can scrape this, and that is accepted
Per ADR-0012: tokens are 10-minute, non-revocable user JWTs (ADR-0005), so no scraper can hold one.
The metrics are readable by a human who logs in and asks. The continuous proof of correctness is
the **test**, not a scrape. Do not "fix" this with a `permitAll` matcher.

## Explicitly rejected — do not build
- **A hold-contention counter** (`tier=redis|postgres` at `HoldService`'s throw sites). Rejected for
  the three reasons at the top. If it is ever revisited, the design that was worked out was: one
  counter, one low-cardinality `tier` tag, one increment per rejected *request*, at the `tryLock`
  and seat-`HELD`-under-lock sites only — never at `SEAT_ALREADY_BOOKED`, which is a stale request
  rather than a race. Note also that counting in `GlobalExceptionHandler` cannot work: ADR-0009
  deliberately merges both tiers into one `SEAT_ALREADY_HELD` code, so the tier is unrecoverable by
  the time the exception arrives.
- **A Prometheus registry / `/actuator/prometheus`.** Rejected in ADR-0012.
- **A Redis fail-open counter.** "Redis is down" is a health signal and ADR-0013 already surfaces it
  there; two sources of truth for one fact is worse than one.

## ADR
**None, deliberately.** Deleting a metric costs nothing, so this fails `domain-modeling`'s
hard-to-reverse test. The surprising part — why zeros are recorded, and what the numbers say about
ADR-0002's prediction — belongs in a comment at the recording site. ADR-0012 already covers the
exposure posture and the unscrapeability.

## Relevant conventions
- **ADR-0002** — lazy expiry is authoritative, the sweep is UX-only reconciliation. This is the ADR
  the metric exists to test the prediction of.
- **ADR-0011** — `expireOverdueHolds()` must still run after `releaseExpiredHeldSeats()` in the same
  transaction; instrumentation must not disturb that.
- **ADR-0012** — exposure posture; metrics are authenticated and unscrapeable by design.
- CLAUDE.md: constructor injection, no `@Autowired` fields; small single-responsibility methods.
- CONTEXT.md non-goals and #19's: no alerting, dashboards, or log aggregation.
